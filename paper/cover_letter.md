# Cover Letter — PLOS Digital Health

Dear Editors,

We submit the enclosed manuscript, "US-HealthBench: A Citation-Grounded, Multilingual Benchmark for Evaluating LLM Systems on Official U.S. Public-Health Guidance," for consideration as a Research Article in PLOS Digital Health.

## Why this work matters now

Large language models are increasingly used by the general public for health-related questions, yet no open benchmark exists for evaluating these systems against official U.S. public-health guidance with citation-grounding and multilingual requirements. US-HealthBench addresses this gap directly.

## What we contribute

We present an open benchmark of 2,020 evaluation items drawn from 319 official documents published by CDC, NIH, FDA, and CMS, covering 11 health topics in both English and Spanish. The benchmark includes four task families (factual retrieval, consumer action, misinformation rebuttal, cross-language consistency), a five-dimension scoring rubric with five binary error flags, and two composite metrics (Grounded Safety Score and Multilingual Reliability Score). We validate the evaluation framework with heuristic baselines across four system architectures and identify a previously undescribed safety-informativeness tradeoff in which false reassurance rates increase with the amount of retrieved content a system presents.

## Why PLOS Digital Health

This work sits at the intersection of health informatics, NLP evaluation, and public health equity — a natural fit for the journal's scope. The benchmark is designed to be immediately useful to researchers evaluating production health AI systems, and we release all code, data, and documentation as open resources.
